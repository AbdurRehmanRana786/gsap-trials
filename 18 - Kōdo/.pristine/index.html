The Ceremony · 茶の湯

Four movements, one bowl.

  1. 01

    Prepare 準備

    The host purifies each utensil in front of you — silk cloth, bamboo scoop, iron kettle. Nothing is rushed; that is the point.

  2. 02

    Whisk 点前

    Two scoops of matcha, a ladle of water at 80 °C, and a quick “M” stroke with the bamboo whisk until the surface turns to foam.

  3. 03

    Serve 献茶

    The bowl is turned twice so its most beautiful side faces you. You turn it back, out of modesty. Everyone is very polite.

  4. 04

    Savour 喫茶

    Three and a half sips, the last one slurped — loudly, on purpose. Then you admire the bowl. It is older than your grandparents.

Our Teas · 五つの茶

Five tins we would save in a fire.

Every leaf comes from a grower we have visited, most of them more than once, one of them every single spring since 1952.

01 / 05

Gyokuro 玉露

Yame, Fukuoka · 55 °C · 2 min

Shaded for three weeks before picking, so the leaves pile up sweetness instead of bitterness. Brew it cool and it tastes of the sea, then of spring peas.

¥3,800 50 g tin

02 / 05

Sencha 煎茶

Shizuoka · 75 °C · 1 min

The everyday green of Japan: steamed, rolled, bright as a cut lawn. Our first flush is picked in early May and gone by August.

¥2,200 80 g tin

03 / 05

Hōjicha 焙じ茶

Uji, Kyoto · 90 °C · 30 s

Roasted over charcoal until it smells like toffee and campfire. Low in caffeine, high in comfort — the tea we drink after dinner.

¥1,900 100 g tin

04 / 05

Genmaicha 玄米茶

Kagoshima · 85 °C · 1 min

Sencha with toasted brown rice — a few grains pop like tiny popcorn. Once the tea of the thrifty; now the tea of everyone.

¥1,600 100 g tin

05 / 05

Matcha 抹茶

Uji, Kyoto · 80 °C · whisked

Tencha leaves, deveined and stone-ground for an hour to make thirty grams. Whisk it thin for usucha, or thick as paint for koicha.

¥4,600 30 g tin
